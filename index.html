This game follows the spirit of the DuBard Association Method (University of Southern Mississippi): one sound at a time, multisensory (hear it, say it, see it, trace it), and nothing appears in a word before it has been taught.

The #1 tip: the phone's voice is only an approximation. Your voice is the real teacher. Say sounds pure and short: /m/ is "mmm", not "muh". /t/ is a tiny tick, not "tuh". Keeping sounds clipped makes blending much easier for kids with dyslexia.
How to play together: have her say the sound out loud while she traces the letter with her finger. That say-while-writing link is the heart of the Association Method.
Little and often beats long sessions. 5–10 minutes a day is perfect. Stop while it's still fun. Repeat earlier letters often — review is a feature, not a failure.
b and d: they live in different worlds on purpose, far apart. When she meets b, the game teaches "b has its belly after the line."

The order of letters (m, a, s, t, p, i, n, o…) lets real words appear fast — after just 4 letters she can blend am, at, mat, sat, Sam.

Share what you're doing here with her therapist so home practice lines up with therapy. This game is a fun supplement, not a replacement.
