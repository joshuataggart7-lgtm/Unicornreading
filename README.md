# A Parent's Quick Guide to the DuBard Association Method

*For helping a young child with dyslexia learn to read at home, alongside professional therapy. Written for a dad, by request. This is a plain-language summary of publicly available descriptions of the method, not official DuBard training — her therapist is the expert on your specific kid.*

---

## Where it comes from

The Association Method was developed by Mildred McGinnis at the Central Institute for the Deaf, then refined by Dr. Etoile DuBard and the staff of the DuBard School for Language Disorders at the University of Southern Mississippi, starting in 1962. It's a structured, phonetic, multisensory approach in the same family as Orton-Gillingham, and it works because it never asks a child to guess. Every skill is built from a smaller skill she already owns.

## The whole method in one paragraph

A child learns one sound at a time, through every sense at once. She hears the sound, watches how the mouth makes it, says it precisely, and writes it while saying it. That saying-while-writing link is the "association" in Association Method. Nothing new is introduced until the current material is fully mastered, and she is never shown a word containing a sound she hasn't been taught. Complexity climbs a slow, reliable ladder: sounds, then sound combinations, then words, then sentences, then questions and stories. Speech is kept slow and articulation precise the whole way, and repetition is treated as the engine of learning rather than a sign of failure.

## The seven big ideas

**1. One new thing at a time.** A single sound is introduced and worked until solid. In the classic method a new sound is written six times on a page, in different colors, and the child reads it, traces it, writes it, picks it out by listening and by watching lips, and recalls it from memory.

**2. Multisensory, always.** Ears, eyes, mouth, and hand fire together on every rep. For a dyslexic brain, the motor and speech channels reinforce the visual channel that isn't cooperating on its own.

**3. The association itself: say it while you write it.** This is the habit to protect at home. When she traces or writes a letter, she says its sound out loud at the same time. Not the letter name, the sound.

**4. Mastery before movement.** The method is unhurried on principle. Children reach "fully mastered" before advancing. At home this means repeating old material often and cheerfully. Review is a feature.

**5. Nothing undecodable.** She should never be asked to read a word containing a sound she hasn't learned. No guessing from pictures, no "what word would make sense here?" Guessing is the habit dyslexia teaches, and this method exists to replace it.

**6. Slow, precise speech.** Adults slow their speech rate so she can process and watch mouths. She's expected to articulate precisely from the start. Precision now buys fluency later.

**7. Cursive first (in the full method).** DuBard teaches writing in cursive from the beginning: the fluid connected motion helps a child feel where a word starts and stops, and cursive letters are harder to reverse than print (a cursive b and d don't mirror each other). Children read print but write cursive; about 95% transition to print easily later. At home, follow whatever her therapist is doing here.

## The ladder she climbs

The progression runs roughly: individual sounds → "drop drills" where two sounds are spoken in sequence (not yet blended) after 8–10 sounds are solid → "cross drills" building syllable patterns (consonant-vowel, vowel-consonant, consonant-vowel-consonant) → a noun-building stage until she owns about 50 words → true sound blending → basic sentences → questions and answers → connected stories, and eventually ordinary books. You don't need to run this whole ladder at home. Knowing it exists tells you where her therapy is heading and why nobody's rushing.

## What a home session looks like

Five to ten minutes, daily-ish, ending while it's still fun. A good shape:

1. **Warm review** (1–2 min). Flash through sounds she already owns. Fast, easy wins first.
2. **The working sound** (2–3 min). Whatever sound therapy is on right now. She hears it, watches your mouth, says it, then traces or writes it big while saying it.
3. **Ears only** (1–2 min). You say a sound, she points to or writes the letter. Then flip it: show a letter, she gives the sound.
4. **Use it** (1–2 min). If she has enough sounds, blend one or two real words made only of sounds she knows. If not, hunt for objects around the house that start with her sound.
5. **Celebrate and stop.** Stop before she wants to.

## Saying sounds correctly (the one skill to practice yourself)

The most common way well-meaning adults sabotage blending is adding "uh" to consonants. "Buh-ah-tuh" can't be blended into "bat"; /b/ /a/ /t/ can. Two families of sounds:

| Type | Sounds | How to say them |
|---|---|---|
| Stretchy (continuous) | m, s, f, n, l, r, v, z, and vowels | Hold them: "mmmm," "ssss" |
| Quick (stops) | b, t, p, d, k/c, g, j, h | Tiny and clipped, no "uh" after |

Practice in a mirror once. If you can say "sun" as "sss-uh-nnn" with no vowel after the s, you've got it.

## Do and don't

Do keep sessions short and end on a win. Do let her see your mouth when you make sounds. Do say sounds while writing, every time. Do repeat mastered material often. Do tell her therapist what you're doing at home so it all lines up.

Don't ask her to guess words from pictures or context. Don't introduce sounds ahead of therapy without checking. Don't treat repetition as remedial or let her see frustration about pace. Don't compare her to siblings or classmates. She's not behind on a normal path; she's on a different path, walking it correctly. And her math tells you exactly how much horsepower is there.

## How the Unicorn Star Reader game maps to this

The game in this folder follows the method's shape: one sound at a time in a deliberate order (m, a, s, t first, so real words appear after just four letters), a multisensory cycle for each letter (hear it, trace it while saying it, identify it by ear, connect it to first sounds of words), mastery gates between worlds, and blending that only ever uses taught sounds. The letters b and d are placed in different worlds, far apart, because they're the classic reversal pair. The phone's voice is an approximation. Your voice, saying clean clipped sounds, is the real model.
